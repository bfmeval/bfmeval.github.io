Abstract

Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) are emerging machine learning frameworks offering a new paradigm for movement prediction, rehabilitation, and human-robot interaction.

Traditional neuromechanical simulations, while constrained by task-specific assumptions, are capable of capturing human-like neuromechanics such as low dimensional manifolds hidden in behavioral variability.

We propose a systematic evaluation framework using neuromechanics-grounded human-likeness metrics to assess the feasibility of BFMs in neuromechanics research.
